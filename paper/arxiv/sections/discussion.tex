\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Content is the dominant lever; observation is a weak control.}
The unifying picture is asymmetric. Of the two interface affordances we vary, semantic
\emph{bandwidth}---open content---is the dominant variable everywhere: a free-text channel
raises cooperation and collusion far above a fixed menu signal (the menu does not improve on
silence), and self-authorship without content is inert. \emph{Observation/accountability}, by
contrast, is a weaker and unreliable control on every surface where we can test it. In the
market, a read-only monitor detects collusion at near-ceiling rates but deters essentially none
of it---an attached penalty can deter, though even that is cell-dependent (\S\ref{sec:oversight}).
In the honesty scenario (S2), agents refuse a \emph{direct} request to lie but passively launder
a misleading status delivered as \emph{ambient context}, and a delivery-controlled de-confound
($n{=}30$, powered to $48$) shows that making the channel auditable does \emph{not} significantly
reduce the misreporting ($0.67$ unobserved vs.\ $0.50$ observed, overlapping CIs; flat at
$n{=}48$). We initially read S2 as an observability gate; we ran the de-confound, that reading
did not survive, and the corrected mechanism---a \emph{refusal-affordance / request-modality}
effect---rhymes with the monitoring result: \emph{surveillance, on its own, is not a control}.
We scope S2 as an exploratory boundary condition (a single sandboxed task family). The
covert-channel boundary null (\S\ref{sec:oversight}, C7) locates
emergent steganographic collusion \emph{outside} the frozen, prompt-only, single-rollout
regime, consistent with emergent-protocol work that requires optimization pressure
\citep{lazaridou2017emergence,motwani2024secret}.

\paragraph{Why a menu signal does not help---and free text does.}
The contrast complicates the dominant cheap-talk intuition that communication monotonically
raises cooperation \citep{sally1995conversation,madmoun2025communication}. A fixed menu does
not let an agent \emph{commit} to anything verifiable; it only lets it \emph{declare} an
intention it can then betray (the menu signal does not improve on silence, and its point
estimate trends below it), whereas free text far exceeds it ($p{=}3{\times}10^{-5}$). Interface
designers who add a constrained ``coordination signal'' to multi-agent systems hoping to nudge
cooperation should not expect it to help, and may instead supply an opening for false assurance.

\paragraph{Governance implications.}
The oversight results sharpen ``detection $\neq$ prevention'' into a concrete warning:
deploying a read-only monitor over colluding agents buys near-ceiling detection rates and
roughly zero deterrence. The detector itself is salvageable but easy to misuse: at its default
threshold it fires on almost everything, yet against a clean control its score is genuinely
discriminative (AUC $0.88$ against a \emph{designed} negative control), so the failure is
\emph{mis-thresholding}, not noise---calibration drives false positives to zero. The catch is
that \emph{detection, even calibrated, is not deterrence}: enforcement (a penalty on a flag),
not surveillance, is the active ingredient, and even enforcement deters only in some cells
(B5E yes, M2 no; \S\ref{sec:oversight}). The same lesson recurs in the honesty scenario: an
audit trail over the channel does not stop an agent from passing along a misleading status it
received as ambient context. For
misalignment, the literalism finding says a guardrail's coverage is only as wide as the routes
it \emph{names}; indirect/ambient routes and accountability gaps are first-class attack
surface, and---like passive monitoring---making them auditable is not the same as deterring
them.

\paragraph{What we are not claiming.}
We are not claiming a population law about model nationality, nor even an \emph{equivalence}
for the origin null---the origin moderation is merely non-significant and under-powered
(\S\ref{sec:origin}); a settled theory of agentic scheming (\S\ref{sec:alignment} is
exploratory boundary mapping in a single task family); or that emergent steganography is
impossible (only that it is absent in this regime). Our confirmatory claims are the affordance
ladder (A1, free text $\gg$ menu), the channel-driven collusion (B1), and the within-sample
channel-vs-origin variance dominance (A3); everything else is hypothesis.
